## The one decision waiting for a human

**B3 says `smallest` is shippable and the default was deliberately not
changed.** See `docs/SUPPRESSION-KEY-FINDINGS.md` §5.

- The confound is real: the shipped score-ordered pool moves when blend weights
  move (0.76% of the fixture pool). Every score-free key is bit-identical under
  the same perturbation.
- `smallest` costs nothing measurable — interval includes zero at every k,
  `ranked@50` unchanged at 58, built 161 → 162.
- **`largest`, which the review proposed, is the only arm with a CI-clear
  loss.** If B3 is adopted, adopt `smallest`.

Flipping `SUPPRESS_SCORE` → `SUPPRESS_SMALLEST` in `sentinel/detect/merge.py`
and `CandidateGenerator` would move every ring-level number in the repository,
so it needs a deliberate re-render and a fresh baseline, not a side effect.

**Until it is flipped, every scorer A/B remains structurally confounded** — that
is what B3 exists to unblock.

## Rules that came out of it

- **A middle-of-funnel gain that does not reach the output is not a product
  improvement.** S1 gave "+14 built, +0 ranked"; B1 gave "p@50 up 70%, distinct
  rings down 58→55". Both are true statements whose first half is the
  misleading one. Report the product-relevant counterpart beside the flattering
  one.
- **A kill criterion referencing the size baseline must quantify over every
  reported k.** A criterion narrower than the rule it encodes keeps returning
  comfortable answers.
- **When a review proposes one member of a family, test the family.** B3's
  proposed key was the worst of three.
- **Two guards, not one, and they must fail differently.** L1's static walk
  answers "did anyone write the name"; the poison test answers "does the answer
  depend on the truth". Neither replaces the other, and both carry negative
  controls.
